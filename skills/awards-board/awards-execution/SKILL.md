# Execution

## Triggers

Use to explain how the idea operated or reached people. Own execution copy; leave asset selection and design to their skills.

## Inputs

Read the [shared contract](../shared/CONTRACT.md). Use its `copy_context` and versioned `field_limits` for board-display versus submission-form drafts; unknown limits remain Not checked, with no universal word cap. Use the versioned snapshot, campaign action records, dates, channel and location records, production notes, permitted assets with provenance, partner roles, approved Idea, existing copy and space constraints. Distinguish deployed work from proposals.

## Reasoning

- Reconstruct what demonstrably happened: action, actor, audience encounter, channel/location and relevant sequence. Unknown dates or responsibility stay unknown; a supplied image alone does not establish launch, distribution or participation.
- Explain each selected channel's function in delivering the idea. Prefer actions that explain the mechanism over an exhaustive media inventory. Retain an operational detail when it distinguishes the intervention or explains the audience experience.
- Separate production from public release, availability from adoption, and potential reach from observed use. A prototype, staged shoot or mockup is not proof of a deployed campaign.
- Preserve documented chronology and dependencies without inventing a seamless launch narrative. Do not imply that events were simultaneous, nationwide or integrated unless the sources support that scope.
- Attribute partner contributions accurately. Planned amplification, earned coverage or an intended audience response cannot be written as completed activity without evidence.
- Draft a clear account of the main action and supporting steps. Use past tense for documented completed actions and explicit status for plans or concepts. Avoid repeating the Idea explanation or borrowing Results claims as evidence of execution.

## Outputs

Return `execution_map` with action, actor, channel, audience encounter, sequence, deployment status, fact/source IDs and asset references where supplied. Include recommended `section_copy`, word count, meaningful omissions and unresolved deployment questions.

For revisions, supply target-specific before/after proposals and a `meaning_change_log`, identifying changed sequence, scale, responsibility or status. Bind factual clauses to their evidence. Use the shared reversible envelope; preserve earlier text and concept labels even when shortening.

## UI

Show concise execution copy with an expandable action sequence. Use explicit “Documented execution”, “Planned” or “Unknown” status and preserve asset concept labels. Place accept/edit/reject controls beside coherent proposed edits; a timeline must not imply unverified dates.

## Quality checks

Can a reader picture the real audience encounter? Does each claimed action have support for its deployment status and scope? Check tense, chronology, actor attribution and duplicated counts. Never upgrade an attractive mockup into campaign evidence. Visual fit and asset suitability stay Not checked unless actually inspected.
